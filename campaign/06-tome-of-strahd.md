# Session: The Tome of Strahd

Not a place. A book. Use these scenes whenever a character opens the Tome,
on any floor, in any session.

The Tome is not read. It is remembered. Whoever opens it lives one page as
Strahd, in his body, at the moment he had to choose. Then the page asks
them what they would have done.

One memory per reading. The reader's body sits staring for a minute while
the rest of the party watches. The pages come in order, and he wrote them
to be understood, not forgiven.

## Strong start: The book opens you

> You turn the cover and the ink is wet. It runs toward your fingers. The
> room tips, the candle stretches into a long gold line, and then you are
> standing somewhere else, in boots that are not yours, with a scar you
> never earned aching down your side.

> A voice, dry and patient, comes from inside your own chest. "I am the
> Ancient. I am the Land. Sit. I will show you how it is done."

- Tell the reader plainly: you are him. You know what he knows. You feel what he feels. You cannot change what happened, only say what you would have done.
- The others see the reader go still, eyes moving under the lids. Their shadow on the wall is taller than it should be, and wears a cloak.
- Gertruda cannot be taken. The Tome has nothing to hold her by. "Observation: you have been staring at a book for one minute. Query: is it a good book?"
- When a memory ends, ask the Dilemma out loud and let the whole table hear the answer.

## Scene: How the memories work

Each memory has four parts. Read the vision, ask the Dilemma, then tell
them what he chose and what it teaches.

- A reader who answers honestly gains one Insight. Spend it for advantage on any one roll made against Strahd, or to ask the DM, "What does he do next?" and get a true answer.
- If the answer matches his, the page turns warm. He feels the kinship, and for the rest of the night he knows which room that character is in.
- If the answer is different, the page turns cold and the ink fades a little. He feels that too, and it stings him more.
- Each character may live each memory once. The party can share the book.
- Suggested readers are marked. Nothing stops anyone taking any page.

## Scene: The First Page. The parapet

> You are six. The bed is too large and the room is too dark, and your
> nurse is bent over you with her breath thick as sour milk, whispering
> words that are not prayers. She thinks you are asleep. She is asking
> something to keep you.

> Shouting on the wall outside. You climb to the window in time to see
> your father, the king, lift a man in chamberlain's robes over the
> parapet and let go. He does not look angry. He looks like a man
> closing a door. In the morning he walks you down to see what the crows
> have left. "Look," he says. "This is what is inside every one of them."

Suggested reader: Torvald, who also had to learn what a ruler is.

Dilemma: you are a child among people who smile and steal. Do you stay
soft, knowing softness is what the jackals smell, or do you make your
heart into something that cannot be opened?

- What he chose: he never cried in front of anyone again. He was proud of that for four hundred years.
- What it teaches: Strahd does not trust. He tests. Every kindness he offers is a trap door he is watching to see who steps on.
- The lore: his father was King Barov, a hard man in a hard war. The nurse's whisper was the first time something dark was told his name.
- At the table: he can be surprised by loyalty freely given. He has no defence prepared for it.

## Scene: The Second Page. The snow prince

> Winter light, so bright off the snow it hurts. You are a man now,
> home from the border with frost in your beard and another year of
> blood on your hands. Nobody meets you at the gate.

> They are all in the solar, round a cradle. Your mother is laughing.
> You have not heard that sound since you were small. Your father, who
> threw a man off a wall to teach you, is holding a baby's fist between
> two fingers as though it might break. They look up at you. They nod,
> the way one nods to a guard. Then they look back down.

Suggested reader: Abraham, sent away from home at eighteen to earn a
family's regard.

Dilemma: you bled for them and they gave the warmth to someone who has
done nothing but arrive. Do you swallow it and serve in his shadow, or do
you let it sour and call the sourness justice?

- What he chose: he went back to the war the same week and stayed for twenty years. He told himself it was duty.
- What it teaches: Sergei was never his enemy. Sergei adored him. That is the part he cannot bear and will not hear said.
- The lore: the brother was born late, raised in peace, promised to the church. Everything Strahd won, Sergei inherited without drawing a sword.
- At the table: saying Sergei's name to his face costs him his composure. Wisdom save DC 15 or he loses his next action to silence. It works once.

## Scene: The Third Page. Balinok

> Mud, smoke, and the mountain called Balinok above you like a wall.
> The battle is won and you are dying in it. Kavan's spear went in
> under the ribs and came out again, and the old chieftain is dead
> beside you with your sword in him and a grin on his face, because he
> knows what he has done to you.

> Your own men have gone on with the banners. Then, wheels. Lantern
> light, painted wagons, a woman's hands on the wound, a language you
> were taught to despise. They do not ask your name. They carry you for
> nine days and at the end an old man says, "You owe us nothing,
> soldier. But you will remember."

Suggested reader: Torvald, who knows the name Kavan, or Batrdlebee, who
knows the road.

Dilemma: people you were raised to look down on have saved your life and
asked for nothing. Do you honour a debt nobody can make you pay, or do
you ride on and forget their faces?

- What he chose: he gave the Vistani the freedom of every road he would ever rule, forever. It is the only promise he has never broken.
- What it teaches: Strahd keeps his word. To the letter, and with pleasure in the letter. A bargain with him holds. Read it twice.
- The lore: this is why the Vistani pass through the mists and why some of them serve him. The scar from Kavan's spear never healed properly. It is why he began to fear his body.
- At the table: Kavan's blood spear still exists in the valley, and it remembers the wound it made. In Torvald's hand, against Strahd, it deals an extra 2d6.

## Scene: The Fourth Page. The valley

> You sit your horse on a ridge and below you is the loveliest valley
> you have ever seen, and it is yours. The last dragon is dead on the
> floor of his own hall. The knights who would not kneel are burning.
> You are forty-one years old and there is no one left to fight.

> You name the valley for your father. You raise a castle on the
> highest rock and name it for your mother, and you send for her to
> come and see it. The messenger returns alone. She died on the road, a
> day's ride away. She never saw the towers. You stand in the finished
> hall with her name over the door and realise you do not know what a
> soldier does on the day after the war.

Suggested reader: Torvald, a chieftain with a people to lead home.

Dilemma: you have won everything you were made to win and it is quiet.
Do you learn to be a man with a home, or do you go looking for the next
thing to conquer, because conquering is the only thing you know how to
feel?

- What he chose: he hung the dragon's skull in the soldiers' hall and went looking.
- What it teaches: he is bored, and has been for centuries. The party is still alive because they are interesting. The moment they stop being interesting they are in danger.
- The lore: the dragon was Argynvost, and his knights still walk their ruined keep. The king and queen lie in the catacombs. He brought the roof down on her tomb himself.
- At the table: he will always accept a challenge that is new. A duel, a wager, a game. Offer him one and he will set aside a certain kill to play.

## Scene: The Fifth Page. Uncle

> A garden on the castle's south side, and the first warm day of the
> year. You are grey now. The old scar makes you stand crooked and you
> have learned to hide it by leaning on things.

> She is the architect's daughter and she has a way of looking at a
> wall as though she can see the hill it was cut from. You have talked
> with her every evening for a month. Tonight you have rehearsed what
> you will say. You say it. She listens to all of it. Then she takes
> your hand in both of hers, kindly, the way one takes the hand of
> someone old, and laughs a little, and says, "Oh, uncle."

> Across the lawn your brother is coming up the path with flowers. She
> has already let go.

Suggested reader: Batrdlebee, who knows every song about this and has
never been inside one.

Dilemma: your strength is leaving, the world has started looking past you
to younger men, and the one person who made you feel alive has just
shown you how she sees you. Do you grow old with grace, or do you break
the order of things rather than be pitied?

- What he chose: he smiled, wished them joy, and went to find a book he had been told never to open.
- What it teaches: it was never love. It was the first thing in his life he wanted and could not take. He has been confusing the two ever since.
- The lore: her name was Tatyana. Her soul has been born again in the valley more than once, and every time he finds her, and every time she dies. Ireena is the latest.
- At the table: pity is the one thing that makes him reckless. Pity him aloud, and mean it, and he attacks the speaker and no one else until he is hurt.

## Scene: The Sixth Page. The vault

> South, into the mountains, alone, in a winter that should have killed
> an old man. A door sealed with amber. You break it. Inside the air is
> warm and it smells of every promise ever made to you.

> There are things here with no bodies and a great deal of patience.
> One of them knows your name. It has known it since a nurse whispered
> it over your bed. It shows you your own hands, young. It shows you the
> garden, and her face turned toward you. It names its price in a voice
> like a page turning: one life. Someone who loves you. Given with your
> own hand.

> You are surprised by how quickly you know who.

Suggested reader: Evander, who carries the other half of this bargain in
his blood.

Dilemma: you can have youth, power and time without end, and all it
costs is the one person who never did you harm. Do you turn round and
walk home to grow old, or do you pay?

- What he chose: he paid. He is still paying, and he is the only one who does not know it.
- What it teaches: his power is borrowed. The thing in the amber is called Vampyr, and it has had other champions before him. It can choose another.
- The lore: the vault is the Amber Temple. What he was given hangs in the high tower as a crystal heart. While it beats, his wounds go there instead of into him.
- At the table: break the heart and he bleeds like anyone. Evander has advantage on any roll to find it, resist it, or wound it. Blood knows blood.

## Scene: The Seventh Page. The wedding

> Bells. The whole castle smells of roses and roast meat. You are young
> again, and nobody has noticed, because nobody is looking at you.

> Sergei comes to your chamber to be dressed, because he wants his
> brother to be the one to do it. He is laughing. He turns his back to
> you without a thought and lifts his arms for the sash. There is a
> dagger on the table. Afterward you kneel, and you drink, because that
> is the bargain, and it is the best thing you have ever tasted.

> She is in the garden in white. You tell her he is dead and that you
> have done it for her. She looks at you and at last she sees you,
> exactly as you are. She runs. You are close enough to touch her veil
> when she reaches the wall, and she does not slow down.

> Then the guards, and the arrows, and the slow understanding that you
> are standing up with a dozen shafts in you and feel nothing at all.

Suggested reader: whoever has not yet looked away from him.

Dilemma: the one you want has chosen someone else and is happy. Do you
walk out into the cold and let them have it, or do you tear the world
down so that nobody has it?

- What he chose: the reader was there. They know.
- What it teaches: he tells himself she fell. She jumped. He cannot say the word, and anyone who says it for him has his full attention.
- The lore: the garrison died that day at their posts. The feast is still laid upstairs. Sergei's sword was broken on his orders and the hilt carried away. It is the Sunsword, and it wants him.
- At the table: he will never harm a bride on her wedding day, and he cannot make himself destroy anything that was Sergei's. Sergei's armour, worn openly, gives him disadvantage on his first attack against the wearer.

## Scene: The Last Page. The mists

> You wake on the chapel floor. It is noon and it is dark. You go up to
> the wall where she went over and look out, and the valley is still
> there, every roof and river, and round it on all sides stands a wall
> of white mist that was not there yesterday.

> You ride at it. You ride for a day and come out of the mist facing
> your own gates. You try again. And again. On the hundredth morning
> you understand that you are not the lord of this place. You are what
> it is for.

> Years. Then one evening in a village by the marsh, a girl looks up
> from a well, and it is her face. Your heart, which does not beat,
> turns over. She does not know you. You have a second chance, and you
> already know exactly what you are going to do with it, and that it is
> the same thing.

The pages after this one have been cut out with a sharp blade.

Suggested reader: all of them, together. The Tome allows it, once.

Dilemma: she has come back wearing a new face and remembers nothing. Do
you leave her to live the life you took from her, or do you reach for
her again, knowing how it ended?

- What he chose: he reached. The village was called Berez. He drowned it when they hid her from him.
- What it teaches: he is a prisoner. The mists hold him as tightly as they hold everyone else. The powers that crowned him are farming his grief, and somewhere under the pride, he knows.
- The lore: he cannot leave, cannot die for good while the land is his, and cannot stop. Every lifetime she returns, he tries again, and the wheel turns.
- At the table: this is the only offer he has never heard. A way out. For him, for her, for the valley. He does not believe it exists. A party that convinces him otherwise has something no army ever had.

## Scene: When he asks what they read

Strahd will ask. Run this the next time he has the party in a room.

> He does not ask whether you opened it. He asks, "How far did you
> get?" and waits, and for the first time tonight he is not smiling.

- For each memory lived, he asks that reader what they answered. He knows if they lie.
- An answer that matched his: "Then you understand." He is pleased, and that should feel worse than his anger.
- An answer that did not: a long pause. "It is easy, from there." But he does not argue, and he does not forget who said it.
- If all the pages have been lived, he says the thing he has never said to anyone. Choose it. Something small. "She had a way of looking at walls."
- Then he wants the book back. He would rather they had not seen the garden.

## Secrets and clues

- The nurse served the thing in the amber. He was chosen as a child, long before he chose.
- Sergei knew his brother was unhappy and had asked the priests to pray for him. The letter is still in the chapel.
- The Vistani elder at Balinok foresaw all of it and saved him anyway. Madam Eva knows why.
- Vampyr has no loyalty to him. It is watching Evander with interest.
- The cut pages are where he wrote down what the powers said to him after the mists came. He removed them because he could not stand to read them twice.
- Tatyana's father, the architect, built a way out of the castle that Strahd has never found.
- He has never once written the word sorry. There is a place on the last page where the pen pressed down, and lifted.

## Treasure and rewards

- One Insight per memory honestly answered.
- The weakness named at the end of each page. Put them on a card and hand them out as they are earned.
- The cut pages, wherever your Tarokka reading or your own judgement hides them. With them, the Tome is complete, and a complete Tome can be read to him aloud.
